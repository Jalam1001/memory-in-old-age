📝 Memory self-tests

Simple versions of tasks used by doctors and researchers. Find a quiet spot, wear your glasses or hearing aids if you use them, and turn sound on (🔊) to hear words read aloud.

⚠️ These are not diagnostic tests. They are informal, unvalidated versions for interest and for tracking yourself over time. Scores are affected by tiredness, mood, distraction, eyesight, hearing and your device. A low score does not mean dementia, and a good score does not rule out a problem. If you're worried, please see a doctor — they can use validated tools such as the MoCA or Mini-Cog (see the write-up).

Digit span

Numbers appear one per second. Type them back. Each success adds a digit; two misses at the same length ends the test.

  • Forward measures short-term memory and attention.
  • Backward (type them in reverse order) measures working memory — holding and rearranging information. Backward spans are usually shorter than forward.

Block tapping (Corsi-style)

Blocks light up one by one with a tone. When they stop, tap the blocks in the same order. This is a visual-spatial version of digit span. Sequences start at 2 blocks and grow; two misses at one length ends the test.

Reaction speed

Processing speed tends to slow with age, and it affects how well we take in new information. It was one of the abilities trained in the ACTIVE study, where benefits lasted 10 years.[11]

Tap the big button (or press the Space bar) to start. When it turns green, tap as fast as you can. 5 tries.

Story recall

Like the "Logical Memory" tasks used by neuropsychologists. Read (or listen to) a short story once, then retell it in your own words. You score a point for each of the 20 "idea units" you include. Come back later for delayed recall.

When to see a doctor

If memory problems are getting worse, affecting daily life, or worrying family or friends, make an appointment. Bring a list of medicines (including over-the-counter ones), and ideally someone who knows you well. Many causes are treatable.
