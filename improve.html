🌱 Improve your memory

Memory has two halves: building a memory (getting it in well) and recalling it (getting it back out). Below are techniques for both, the lifestyle habits with the best evidence, therapies and treatments (each labelled with the kind of evidence behind it), and a simple daily routine.

🧱 Building memories (encoding)

🎯

Pay attention on purpose

Much "forgetting" is really not noticing. Pause and say it out loud: "I'm putting my glasses on the bookshelf."

🔗

Link it (elaboration)

Connect new facts to what you know. Meeting Mrs. Fisher? Picture her holding a fishing rod. Ask "why?" and "how does this relate to me?"

📦

Chunk it

Group items into small sets. 0 7 9 1 2 3 4 5 6

🖼️

Words + pictures

"Dual coding": combine words with images. Sketch it, or imagine it vividly — colour, size, sound, smell.

🏛️

Memory palace

The method of loci: imagine placing each item along a familiar route — milk on the doormat, eggs on the sofa, bread in the bath. Walk the route in your mind to recall.

🐢

Slow down, one thing at a time

Processing speed slows with age,[7] so give yourself more time and turn off distractions (TV, radio) when learning something new.

🔎 Recalling memories (retrieval)

❓

Test yourself

Retrieval practice beats re-reading: in one study, people who practised recalling a passage kept 61% after a week vs. 40% for those who re-read it.[10] Close the book and ask yourself what you remember.

📅

Space it out

Review after 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month. Use the planner below.

🚪

Go back to the context

Forgot why you walked into a room? Go back to where the thought started. Place and mood are powerful cues.

🔤

Alphabet search

Stuck on a name? Run through A, B, C… The first letter often unlocks it.

😌

Relax and let it come

Stress blocks recall. "Tip-of-the-tongue" words often pop up a few minutes later once you stop straining.

📝

Use aids without guilt

Calendars, pill organisers, phone reminders, sticky notes and a fixed "landing spot" for keys free up your memory for what matters.

📅 Spaced review planner

Want to remember something — a poem, names of new neighbours, a recipe? Type it in and get your review dates.

🏷️ How to read the evidence labels

Each approach below has a label for what kind of benefit the studies actually showed. A better score on a test is not the same as an easier everyday life, or as slower decline over years.

📈 Better test scoresDid better on memory or thinking tests in a trial.
🏠 Better everyday functioningManaged daily activities better (as measured or reported in a trial).
🐢 Slower declineLost thinking skills more slowly over time than a comparison group.
➖ Not shownA trial looked for this benefit and did not find it, or it hasn't been tested yet.
🔗 Linked to risk (observational)Linked to dementia risk in population studies. This does not prove that changing it helps.
🔧 Can reverse it, if it's the causeTreating the cause can make the memory problem go away.

🩺 Therapies and treatments

These are given or arranged by health professionals. Most are for people with diagnosed dementia, not for everyday forgetfulness. Ask your doctor what fits your situation.

☀️ A brain-healthy day

Morning

Daylight and a 20–40 minute brisk walk. Look at your calendar and plan the day.

Mid-morning

15 minutes of something challenging: a new language, an instrument, a puzzle, or the memory games.

Lunch

Vegetables, beans, whole grains, fish or nuts — and conversation with someone.

Afternoon

A social activity or hobby. Strength or balance exercise 2–3 days a week.

Evening

Recall your day: what you ate, who you spoke to, one new thing you learned (that's retrieval practice!). Do any spaced reviews.

Night

Same bedtime each night, a dark, quiet room, and 7–9 hours if you can.
