🌿 Traditional remedies: rosemary & gotu kola

Two plants with a long traditional link to memory. Here is what was actually tested in people, what the results were, and what to watch out for.

ℹ️ This page is information, not a recommendation. It gives no doses and makes no treatment claims. Neither plant has been shown to prevent or treat memory decline. Before trying any herb, oil or supplement, talk to your doctor or pharmacist and ask them to check for interactions with your medicines.

🌿 Rosemary🍃 Gotu kola💬 Before you try anything
🌿

Rosemary, in short

Two small studies found short-term effects (minutes to hours) on speed in thinking tasks. Neither tested whether rosemary slows memory decline. More was not better: the highest dose of dried leaf made memory speed worse.[1][2]

🍃

Gotu kola, in short

A 2017 review and meta-analysis of randomised trials found no significant cognitive benefit compared with placebo.[4] It has been linked to rare cases of liver injury.[5]

🌿 Rosemary (Rosmarinus officinalis)

Traditional use. Rosemary is a kitchen herb with long-standing traditional reputations for the mind. Those reputations are why researchers have tested it for cognitive decline in older people.[2]

It matters which form. Smelling rosemary aroma, swallowing dried leaf, and concentrated essential oil are very different things. Don't treat a result for one as if it applies to the others.

FormWhat the study testedWhat it foundLimits
👃 Inhaled aroma
rosemary essential oil diffused into the air		 20 healthy volunteers did arithmetic (serial subtraction) and visual information-processing tasks in a cubicle filled with rosemary aroma. Blood was taken afterwards to measure an absorbed aroma compound, 1,8-cineole.[1] People with higher blood levels of 1,8-cineole did better on speed and accuracy.[1] One session with 20 people. It looked at a link within the group (a correlation) and measured performance right away. It didn't study older adults with memory problems or long-term use.[1]
🌱 Dried leaf powder, swallowed 28 older adults (average age 75) in a randomised, placebo-controlled, double-blind crossover trial. Each person had a placebo and four different single doses on separate visits a week apart. They were tested 1 to 6 hours after each dose.[2] The lowest dose (the one closest to normal cooking amounts) improved "speed of memory" compared with placebo. The highest dose made it significantly worse. Other thinking measures showed some harmful effects, though less consistently.[2] Short-term only: single doses, measured over a few hours. The authors call for longer studies.[2]
⚗️ Concentrated essential oil Neither study gave people essential oil to swallow. In the aroma study, the oil was only diffused into the air.[1] No evidence here supports swallowing or applying rosemary oil for memory. Essential oils are concentrated, poorly regulated, and can be poisonous if misused (see below).[3]

Bottom line: rosemary studies mainly measured short-term effects, from minutes to a few hours after one exposure. They don't show that rosemary prevents, slows or treats memory decline.

⚠️ Never swallow essential oils

America's Poison Control says misuse of essential oils can cause serious poisoning:[3]

  • Some essential oils are poisonous if swallowed or absorbed through the skin, and many can cause rashes.
  • If someone chokes while swallowing an oil and some enters the lungs, it can cause pneumonia.
  • Products are not well regulated, so it can be hard to know what's in the bottle: the plant species, the concentration, or contaminants.
  • Essential oils may interact with medications.
  • Children are especially at risk, so lock oils away from children and pets.

If someone swallows an essential oil, use the webPOISONCONTROL online tool or call Poison Control at 1-800-222-1222 (US, free, 24 hours a day).[3]

🍃 Gotu kola (Centella asiatica)

Traditional use. Also called Asian pennywort, gotu kola is a flowering plant native to tropical Southeast Asia and Australia. It is eaten as a food and used in traditional medicine.[5] It has been used as an herbal "brain tonic" for mental disorders and to enhance memory.[4]

🔬

What was tested

A 2017 systematic review pooled randomised controlled trials: 5 of gotu kola alone and 6 of products containing it, each compared with placebo.[4]

📉

What it found

No significant differences from placebo in any area of thinking (cognitive domain). There were some mood effects: more alertness and less anger 1 hour after taking it. The authors say there is "not strong evidence" for cognitive benefit.[4]

⚠️

Safety

The trials in the review reported no side effects.[4] But the US LiverTox database says that while gotu kola is generally regarded as safe, it has been linked to rare cases of acute liver injury with jaundice.[5]

The review also points out that products vary a lot in dose, plant preparation and standardisation, which makes the results hard to compare.[4]

💬 Before you try any herb, oil or supplement

  • Talk to your doctor or pharmacist and bring the actual product, so they can check for interactions with your medicines and your health conditions (for example, liver problems).
  • The US National Institute on Aging warns that pills and supplements promising to improve memory may be unsafe, a waste of money, or both, and might interfere with other medical treatments.[6]
  • If memory is worrying you, see a doctor first. Some causes are treatable (see treatable causes and therapies and treatments).
