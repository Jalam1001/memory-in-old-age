🎮 Memory games

Six games that exercise different kinds of memory. Pick a level, turn the sound on 🔊, and play a few rounds each day. Your best scores are saved on this device.

🌿 Every game starts in Relaxed pace: no clocks, and you study until you're ready. Switch any game to ⏱️ Timed if you want a challenge. Best scores are kept separately for each pace.

Games are a fun way to stay mentally active. Research suggests the benefits of brain training are mostly for the skills you practise, so mix games with exercise, social time and learning new things. 📚 See what the research says about each game type →

      What does the score measure?

      Your score is the percentage of letters you filled in yourself, without using "Reveal". Checking your answers is free. Crosswords exercise word finding (retrieving words from memory from a clue), spelling and general knowledge. It's a measure of how this puzzle went for you, not a test of memory health.

      🗂️ Spaced Recall names · facts · long-term memory

      Add names or facts you want to remember — a new neighbour, a grandchild's teacher, a phone number, where the spare key is. The game asks you about each one at growing intervals: 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month, 2 months. Get one wrong and it starts again from 1 day. No timer.

      🔒 Everything you type stays in this browser on this device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

      What do the stage and score measure?

      Stage (1–7) shows how long an item has stayed in your memory: each correct review moves it up a stage and pushes the next review further away. Stage 7 means you remembered it after a 2-month gap. Accuracy is the percentage of reviews you got right. A session score is the percentage you recalled correctly the first time you were asked. Spaced retrieval — practising recall at increasing gaps — is a technique used in memory rehabilitation.

      🃏 Picture Pairs visual memory

      Turn over two cards at a time. If they match, they stay face up. Find all the pairs. There's no rush — the timer is off unless you turn it on.

      Moves: 0Time: 0sBest: –
      What does the score measure?

      Moves = how many pairs of cards you turned over. Fewer moves means you remembered where you'd seen each picture (visual-spatial memory). The smallest possible number of moves equals the number of pairs, but that needs luck — about double is a good game. Best scores are kept separately for each board size.

      🔁 N-Back working memory

      Letters appear (and are spoken) one at a time. Press MATCH (or the M key) when the letter is the same as the one N steps back. In Relaxed pace, each letter waits for you: press MATCH or No match → (N key). In 1-back that means "same as the last one".
